### Evaluation Criteria — Completeness
The diagram should present all relevant components of the architecture, including any interactions between them. Note any missing components or interactions and suggest what should be added to provide a comprehensive view.

### Rating Scale
- **Exemplary Completeness**: The diagram and description provide an exhaustive and coherent depiction of all architectural components and their interactions. The representation covers every major subsystem, interface, and dependency necessary for full comprehension. No meaningful omissions are detected.
- **Acceptable Completeness**: The materials are generally comprehensive but omit a few minor components or secondary interactions. These omissions do not significantly hinder understanding but should be addressed to ensure a fully representative view of the system.
- **Insufficient Completeness**: Several important architectural elements or interactions are missing or underrepresented. The omissions result in a partial understanding of the system's structure and behavior. Significant additions or refinements are needed to achieve completeness.
- **Deficient Completeness**: The diagram and/or description lack substantial portions of the architecture. Major components and relationships are absent, preventing an accurate or meaningful interpretation of the system. Extensive reconstruction is required to present a complete architectural view.
