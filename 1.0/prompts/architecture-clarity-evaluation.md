### Evaluation Criterion — Clarity

Clarity is about how easily a reader (technical or semi-technical) can understand the architecture from the diagram and its description.

In particular, evaluate:

1. **Element Identification**  
   - Are ALL relevant elements (services, databases, queues, external actors, etc.) clearly labeled with meaningful names?

2. **Reading Flow**  
   - Is there an implicit or explicit starting point and progression for reading the diagram (e.g., from user to backend, from top to bottom, from left to right)?
   - Is the main flow of requests/events understandable?

3. **Symbol and Label Readability**  
   - Are the symbols, labels, and connections visually clear (no excessive clutter, overlapping arrows, unreadable text)?

---

### Rating Scale (Clarity)

- **Exemplary Clarity**: The diagram and description are exceptionally clear. Elements are well-labeled, the main flows are obvious, and the reader can quickly form a correct mental model.
- **Acceptable Clarity**: Overall understandable, but minor issues (e.g., occasional vague labels, slightly confusing flows) may slow down comprehension.
- **Insufficient Clarity**: Multiple weaknesses (unclear labels, confusing flows, difficult to see where to start/finish) significantly hinder understanding.
- **Deficient Clarity**: The diagram/description fail to communicate a coherent architecture; it is very hard to understand what the system does or how components interact.
