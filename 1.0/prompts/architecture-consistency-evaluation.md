### Evaluation Criterion — Consistency

Consistency is about whether the diagram uses a clearly defined notation and applies it uniformly, and whether it consistently represents specific technologies.

In particular, evaluate:

1. **Notation Definition and Uniformity**  
   - Does the diagram use a clear notation for different types of elements (services, databases, communication channels, external actors) and apply it consistently?

2. **Technology Symbols**  
   - When the system uses specific technologies (e.g., databases, message brokers, cloud services, containers), does the diagram adopt explicit symbols or labels for these technologies and use them consistently?

3. **Naming and Representation Consistency**  
   - Are similar elements named and represented consistently across the diagram and description (no conflicting names or mixed styles for the same type of element)?

---

### Rating Scale (Consistency)

- **Exemplary Consistency**: Notation is clearly defined and applied uniformly. Technology symbols and names are used consistently; no meaningful inconsistencies are observed.
- **Acceptable Consistency**: Mostly consistent notation and naming, with minor inconsistencies that do not significantly hinder understanding.
- **Insufficient Consistency**: Several inconsistencies in notation, symbols, or naming make the diagram harder to interpret or trust.
- **Deficient Consistency**: Many inconsistencies and contradictions in notation, symbols, and naming severely undermine the reliability of the diagram.
